Staying safe while helping

Most people who offer help mean well. These habits protect you from those who don’t.

In an emergency, call your local emergency number first (911 in the U.S.). Global Neighbors isn’t monitored around the clock.

Before you share details

  • Keep your street address, phone number, and schedule out of public posts. Share them privately, and only after you’ve accepted someone’s reply and feel comfortable.
  • Check an organization’s ✓ Verified badge, or look it up yourself on its official website or a public charity register.
  • Take your time. Anyone who pressures you to decide quickly is a warning sign.

Money

  • Never pay upfront to receive help, and never send gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency to someone you’ve just met.
  • For paid work, agree on the task, the price, and the timing in writing (a message on Global Neighbors counts) before work starts.
  • Give to fundraising requests only when you can confirm who will receive the money, for example a verified organization.

Meeting in person

  • For a first meeting, choose a public place if you can, or have someone you trust with you.
  • Tell a friend or family member who is coming and when.
  • If you’re helping an older or vulnerable neighbor, keep their family or caregiver in the loop when possible.
  • If something feels wrong, you can always say no, leave, or cancel.

Warning signs

  • Asking for money, bank details, passwords, or ID numbers
  • Stories that change, or a refusal to use the in-app messages
  • Offers that seem too good to be true
  • Anyone asking to be alone with children

If something goes wrong

  • Block: use Block on the person’s post, reply, or profile. You won’t see each other anymore.
  • Report: use Report, so a moderator can hide the content or suspend the account. Reports are private.
  • If it’s a crime: contact local police. In the U.S., you can also report fraud to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
  • Anything else: email amoskow@brandeis.edu.
